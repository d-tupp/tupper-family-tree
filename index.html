Family history

The first Tuppers in Nova Scotia

Everything here comes from the cited sources. Where sources disagree, both versions are given.

From Sussex to Cape Cod

The family's American founder, Thomas Tupper (b. 1578, Bury, Sussex), settled at Sandwich in Plymouth Colony in 1637. There he served as a deputy to the General Court for about 20 years and as a selectman, and he preached as a layman among the Wampanoag (searchtrees, citing Thomas Tupper and His Descendants; Calnek). Calnek traces the line from him through Thomas, Thomas and Eliakim to Eliakim Tupper Jr. (b. 1711, Sandwich). Eliakim Jr. married Mary Bassett, daughter of William Bassett of Sandwich, and later lived at Lebanon, Connecticut, where he held the titles of Captain and Deacon and served as a representative.

The Planter migration (c. 1760)

After the Acadians were expelled in 1755, Governor Charles Lawrence offered their farmland to New England settlers, known as the "Planters". Cornwallis and Horton were among the first townships created, and the Nova Scotia Archives holds their 1760 to 1761 grantee registers and land books (NS Archives: Township records). About 1760, Eliakim Jr. (recently remarried), his brother Elias, and others from Connecticut moved to "lands granted to them at Cornwallis". Eliakim died there during the first winter, on 28 Feb 1761 (another source cited gives 28 Jan 1761). Most of the family stayed (douglasjgraham.net, citing Tupper family genealogies). Elias's sons later settled at Clark's Ferry, now Tupperville, in Annapolis County (Calnek).

Charles Tupper (1748 to 1821), farmer of Cornwallis

Eliakim's youngest son, Charles, was born at Plymouth, MA, on 19 Aug 1748 and came to Cornwallis as a boy. He married Elizabeth West (b. 1754, Rochester, MA) in Cornwallis on 24 Oct 1771. FamilySearch lists at least 11 sons and 4 daughters, among them Thomas (1774 to 1856), Dustin's ancestor (an earlier Thomas, born 1772, died as an infant). He died on 29 Apr 1821, and FamilySearch records his burial at Old Morristown Cemetery (FamilySearch). The Dictionary of Canadian Biography describes him as "a farmer" and says the family was Presbyterian before coming under Baptist influence (DCB).

Connection to Sir Charles Tupper

Charles and Elizabeth's son Rev. Charles Tupper DD (1794 to 1881) was a leading Maritime Baptist minister and editor, and a founder of Horton Academy, which became Acadia University. His son Sir Charles Tupper (1821 to 1915) became Premier of Nova Scotia and Prime Minister of Canada (DCB; Calnek). Since Dustin descends from Charles's son Thomas (1774), Sir Charles was a first cousin of Thomas Handley Tupper. Sir Charles's father was Dustin's great-great-great-great-great-granduncle. This relationship follows from FamilySearch and WikiTree listing Thomas (1774) as a son of Charles and Elizabeth. A primary record would make it firm.

Down the Valley

Thomas Tupper (1774 to 1856) married Jerusha Scovil/Schofield. Their son Thomas Handley Tupper (1810 to 1889; Old Kingston Cemetery) married Margaret Phinney, from a Granville Planter family. His son Lot Phinney Tupper (1834/36 to 1923) and Elizabeth Ann Taylor are buried in Morristown Cemetery. So are William John's son Durwood and his son Neil, Dustin's grandfather. That is about 260 years of Tuppers in Kings County.

Uncertainties

  • The exact land-grant lots for Eliakim and Charles have not been found yet. The NS Archives grantee register (RG 1 vol. 360) and the Cornwallis land books are the place to check.
  • Charles's death: 29 Apr 1821 (FamilySearch, WikiTree) versus "after 1837" (one secondary source).
  • Eliakim's death: 28 Feb versus 28 Jan 1761.
  • Eleanor Tupper's Tupper Genealogy, 1578 to 1971 (1972) and Eaton's History of Kings County (1910) are the standard references but were not consulted directly.
